# Infrastructure conventions

This file is loaded into every Kiro session in this repository. It is the
knowledge that used to live in the heads of two people on the infrastructure
team, and its absence is why an agent with no platform context produces
confident, syntactically valid, wrong Terraform.

## The one rule that matters

**Never write Terraform in this repository.** Infrastructure is requested by
writing or editing a `services/<name>/service.yaml` file, validated against
`platform/schemas/service.v1.json`. The platform renders Terraform from that
manifest. If a developer asks for something the schema cannot express, say so
and suggest the closest supported option, or point at the `overrides` escape
hatch. Do not reach for raw HCL as a workaround.

## Why the contract exists

The schema is small on purpose. A generated manifest has a handful of
enumerated fields, so a reviewer can read the whole diff in ten seconds and a
policy engine can reason about it exactly. Generated Terraform has none of
those properties.

## Environments

| Environment | Account | Purpose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dev` | 111122223333 | Everyday development | Budget capped. Destroyed and rebuilt freely. |
| `staging` | 111122223333 | Pre-production verification | Mirrors prod topology at smaller size. |
| `prod` | 111122223333 | Customer traffic | Change requires PR approval from a second person. |

The demo runs all three in one account, separated by tags and naming. A real
deployment separates them by account with SCPs at the organisation level.

## Database sizes

Developers think in size and purpose, not instance classes. The manifest takes
a t-shirt size and the module maps it:

| Size | Instance class | Rough monthly cost | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `small` | `db.t4g.micro` | ~$15 | Dev, staging, low-traffic services |
| `medium` | `db.t4g.small` | ~$30 | Most production services |
| `large` | `db.t4g.medium` | ~$60 | Busy production services |
| `xlarge` | `db.r7g.large` | ~$240 | Production only, and rarely |

`xlarge` is not permitted outside production. This is enforced in
`platform/policies/service.rego`, not by convention, because conventions are
not enforceable against an agent.

## Backups and retention

Production databases keep a minimum of 7 days of automated backups. Dev may use
any retention including none. Retention is a schema field so that the policy can
deny bad values with an explanation rather than the module silently overriding
them.

## What the module gives you without being asked

Every service gets a CloudWatch dashboard, alarms on error rate and latency, an
Application Signals SLO, log groups with a retention policy, ADOT tracing and
correlation IDs. Nobody requests these and nobody would build them by hand.
They are a property of having used the platform.

Do not suggest adding observability to a service. It is already there. If a
developer wants to change the SLO target or log retention, those are the only
two knobs, under `spec.observability`.

## Secrets

Database credentials are generated by the module and written to Secrets
Manager at `/<environment>/<service>/db`. They are never placed in the manifest,
never in environment variables in plaintext, and never in the repository. The
task role is granted read access to its own secret and nothing else.
